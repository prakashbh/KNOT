01

Explain this work in 2-3 sentences.

This research explored how a problem-based learning and computational thinking integrated course could support knowledge construction among computer science students. It brought together classroom design, student work, survey responses, and course performance to develop the KNOT framework for designing learning experiences.

02

What does KNOT stand for?

KNOT means “Knowledge is Not a One-time Transformation.” It describes learning as a continuing process in which understanding is reshaped through questions, inquiry, application, and reflection.

03

What is the central research question?

The study asks how a PBL and computational thinking integrated course framework influences knowledge construction among computer science students.

04

Why was Design and Analysis of Algorithms chosen?

The course asks students to understand concepts, select and apply algorithms, reason about efficiency, and solve unfamiliar problems, making it a useful setting for examining knowledge construction.

05

Who took part in the main study?

Undergraduate computer science students taking the Design and Analysis of Algorithms course took part. The number of students represented differs across evidence sources because participation and submission were not identical for every activity.

06

What research design was used?

The main study used a concurrent mixed-methods design, bringing quantitative and qualitative evidence together to examine different aspects of the same course experience.

07

What kinds of evidence were analysed?

The study used course and lab scores, concept-understanding responses, project evaluations, student survey responses, and liminality case studies with reflections.

08

How many survey responses are reported?

Chapter 9 reports 124 survey responses for the cleaned survey dataset used in the analysis.

09

Why do the participant counts vary across chapters?

The work includes three stages with three different student batches. Within each stage, source counts vary because not every student completed every form or submitted every project, and some records are blank, repeated, or unavailable.

10

Which learning outcomes are central to the study?

The study focuses on self-directed learning, problem-solving, and transfer of knowledge as related aspects of knowledge construction.

11

How was qualitative evidence analysed?

The qualitative analysis used a framework approach with inductive and deductive coding, combining ideas from the research questions with patterns found in students’ responses.

12

What are the liminality case studies?

They are 16 student-created case studies that connect everyday, natural, historical, or social examples with algorithmic ideas and design techniques. Their reflections are used to examine students’ accounts of learning transitions.

13

How did the project work introduce students to problems?

Student teams were asked to plan a proposed smart city on a 1,500-square-kilometre area, using a white paper on streets as context and addressing layout, population, budget, revenue, and sustainability.

14

How did computational thinking appear in the course?

Students worked with practices such as problem formulation, decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithm design, and efficiency analysis across lessons, labs, case studies, and projects.

15

What does PBL contribute to the course?

Problem-based learning gives students a problem or context to investigate, discuss, and use as they develop and apply relevant concepts and methods.

16

What is the KNOT Framework?

It is a practical classroom design guide organised around four moves: Kick off, Navigate, Observe, and Tie back. It helps teachers plan a connected learning experience from an opening question through inquiry and reflection.

17

What happens in each KNOT phase?

Kick off opens curiosity with a question or problem; Navigate supports investigation; Observe makes student thinking and learning visible; Tie back connects the experience to concepts, application, and further questions.

18

How does the framework guide assessment?

Assessment is planned to make students’ reasoning, decisions, applications, and reflections visible during and after learning, rather than relying only on a final answer.

19

Can this study show that the framework caused learning gains?

The study reports evidence of student perceptions and course performance within the designed course. Its findings should be interpreted as a connected account of this setting, not as proof that the framework alone caused an outcome.

20

How can another teacher use KNOT?

A teacher can begin with a meaningful question, plan how learners will investigate it, decide what evidence will make their thinking visible, and close by connecting the experience to concepts and future use.
