Workshop 01 First faculty review

KNOTTY CLASS at Sphoorthy Engineering College

A two-hour introduction to KNOT and KNOTTY CLASS. Faculty walked through the framework, received the guide PDF, and tried preparing a class plan for their own subjects.

Date21 November 2026
Time2:00–4:00 p.m.
VenueSphoorthy Engineering College (Autonomous), Hyderabad
Attendance65 faculty members

Across teaching domains

Faculty brought KNOT into many subject areas

The course areas recorded in the feedback span business and economics, several computing fields, engineering, mathematics, and physics. This breadth is a key part of the workshop: faculty considered how the same planning framework could support classes in different disciplines.

Business and economics

Economics: Demand and Supply; Business Economics.

Computing and information technology

Cloud Computing, including data-centre networking; DBMS; Data Structures; Machine Learning; AI and Machine Learning; Deep Learning; NLP; Data Science; Algorithms and Algorithm Design and Analysis; PPS; Object-Oriented Programming through Java; Web Programming; Software Engineering, including the Spiral Model; COA; SPPM; IRS; NIC AIML; and CSE-CS.

Engineering and applied design

Civil Engineering, including Strength of Materials; Electronics; and PCB Design.

Mathematics and physical sciences

Taylor Series and Advanced Engineering Physics (AEP).

Course labels such as PPS, COA, SPPM, IRS, and NIC AIML are retained as recorded in the feedback sheet.

What participants noticed

Practical, student-centred, and adaptable

01

More active learning

Participants valued the practical, student-centred approach and the chance to move beyond lecture-based teaching.

“Practical, student-centred learning rather than depending entirely on lecture-based teaching.”
02

Examples that orient

Real-time examples and a guided walk-through helped participants understand how to begin using the framework.

“Good explanation with real-time examples and orientation.”
03

Quicker planning

Some participants saw KNOTTY CLASS as a way to prepare lesson plans more quickly, including for subjects they were teaching for the first time.

“Excellent framework, very useful, time saving.”

Across the feedback, faculty saw KNOTTY CLASS as a practical way to shape an engaging class plan. The range of course areas shows that the workshop invited educators to explore its use across disciplines, not only within business or computing.

Faculty names are not displayed. Comments are presented without personal identifiers.
